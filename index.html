Welcome to My Baking Journal!

Welcome to my little corner of the baking world!

Whether you're just getting started or you've been baking for years, there's always something new to learn. I've spent years experimenting with recipes, making mistakes, and discovering little tricks that make a big difference.

I created this journal to share some of those tips, lessons, and experiences with you. From baking basics to cake decorating, we'll cover a little bit of everything!

Today's Quick Baking Tip: Measure your flour correctly!

Did you know that scooping flour directly from the bag with your measuring cup can pack in more flour than your recipe needs?

Instead, fluff your flour, spoon it gently into your measuring cup, and level it off with a straight edge. Or, if you're like me... grab a kitchen scale and measure! It's my favorite way to get consistent results every time.

A little extra flour can be the difference between a soft, fluffy cake and a dry, dense one.

xo, Melinda
Baking Journal · Tip no. 02 · Nov 6, 2026

Room Temperature Butter Isn't What You Think!

Did you know? Room temperature butter shouldn't be greasy, shiny, or so soft that it's practically melting!

For most cakes and cookies that use the creaming method, butter should be around 65–68°F (18–20°C). It should feel cool to the touch and give slightly when pressed with your finger.

Why does it matter?

Butter that's too warm won't hold air as effectively during creaming, which can affect the texture and rise of your baked goods.

My Baking Tip

If your kitchen is warm, especially here in Texas, don't rely on leaving butter on the counter for a set amount of time. Check its temperature and texture instead!

xo, Melinda
Baking Journal · Tip no. 03 · Nov 6, 2026

Don't Overmix That Batter!

We've all been there. You're making a cake, everything is going great, and you figure, what's another minute or two of mixing?

Well, believe it or not, that extra mixing might be doing more harm than good!

One thing I've learned over the years is that more mixing doesn't always mean a better cake.

Once you add your flour, you want to be careful not to overmix. The more you work that batter, the more gluten develops, and that can leave you with a cake that's dense or tough instead of soft and fluffy.

Here's My Tip!

When adding your dry ingredients, keep your mixer on low and mix just until everything comes together. I also like to stop and scrape down the sides and bottom of my bowl because, trust me, flour loves hiding down there!

And don't worry if your batter isn't perfectly silky smooth. Depending on the recipe, a few tiny lumps are perfectly fine.

Of course, some cake recipes use different mixing methods, so always pay attention to what your recipe calls for.

Remember, sometimes less really is more!

xo, Melinda

Things I Wish I Knew When I Started Baking

Not every dessert needs to be elaborate or perfectly decorated to be memorable.

Over the years, I've learned that sometimes the simplest desserts leave the biggest impression.

When you're a baker, it's easy to get caught up in making everything look perfect. The smooth buttercream, the intricate decorations, the picture-perfect finish. And while I love creating beautiful desserts, I've realized that sometimes we put way too much pressure on ourselves.

Because at the end of the day, it's not always about how fancy something looks. It's about how good it tastes, the memories it creates, and the joy it brings to the people enjoying it.

Sometimes a simple homemade cake shared with family means more than the most elaborate dessert on the table.

A dessert doesn't have to be perfect to be special.

So here's your reminder to enjoy the process, embrace simplicity, and never underestimate the power of something made with love.

Your Crumb Coat Doesn't Have to Be Pretty!

Can we talk about crumb coats for a second? Because I think we put WAY too much pressure on making them look perfect!

Here's the thing: your crumb coat isn't supposed to be pretty. Its main job is to trap those pesky crumbs and create a base for your final layer of buttercream.

BUT... that doesn't mean you should just slap some buttercream on and call it a day!

You still want to aim for a level top, fairly even sides, and a consistent shape. The shape of your cake at this stage will definitely affect how much work you'll need to do when applying your final coat.

Think of it this way: the better your foundation, the easier your final coat will be!

So don't stress over every little line, bump, or imperfection. Focus on getting your cake reasonably level and even.

Quick Crumb-Coating Tip!

Start with a chilled cake! Cold cake layers are generally firmer and easier to work with, which helps minimize crumbs and makes applying buttercream much easier.

Use a small amount of buttercream to apply a thin, even coat. And here's another little trick: keep a separate bowl of buttercream for your crumb coat so you don't accidentally transfer crumbs into the buttercream you'll use for your final coat!

Once you're happy with the shape, chill your cake until the crumb coat is firm before moving on.

Remember, your crumb coat doesn't have to be perfect, but it should make your next step easier, not harder!

Sometimes It's Not Your Buttercream Recipe!

Ever make the same buttercream recipe you've used a hundred times, only for it to suddenly give you problems? Trust me, I've been there!

Before you start changing ingredients or adding more powdered sugar, check the temperature!

Buttercream that's too warm can become soft, greasy, and difficult to work with. Too cold? It can become stiff, difficult to spread, and even pull at your cake when smoothing.

And let's not forget about your kitchen temperature! What works perfectly in a cool kitchen might behave completely differently on a hot Texas afternoon.

Quick Buttercream Troubleshooting Chart

ProblemWhat to try
Too warm or greasyRefrigerate briefly, then remix
Too cold or firmLet it warm up gradually
Too stiff to spreadAdd water or milk, up to 1 tbsp at a time
Too loose or runnyChill first, then remix
Still too loose after chillingAdd sifted powdered sugar, ¼ cup at a time
Air bubblesMix slowly and press buttercream against the bowl with a spatula
Not holding piping detailsCheck temperature and consistency before adding ingredients

A little tip: Make adjustments gradually! Adding too much liquid or powdered sugar can quickly turn one problem into another. And trust me, nobody wants to spend their afternoon fixing buttercream that was perfectly fine five minutes ago!

One thing I've learned over the years is that knowing how your buttercream should feel is just as important as knowing how to make it.

So before you toss that batch or start searching for a new recipe, give your buttercream a little temperature check. Sometimes that's all it needs!

xo, Melinda
Baking Journal · Tip no. 07 · Nov 6, 2026

Dark Buttercream Gets Darker as It Sits!

Ever try making red buttercream, only to end up with something that looks more like hot pink?

Check out the tips below before you reach for more food coloring!

One thing I've learned over the years is that buttercream colors can deepen as they sit, especially darker shades like red, navy, and black. Sometimes all you need is a little patience!

A Few Tips for Better Buttercream Colors!

  • Start with less coloring. You can always add more, but you can't take it out!
  • Make your colors ahead of time. Whenever possible, let darker shades develop for several hours or overnight in the refrigerator. You might be surprised how much the color deepens!
  • Short on time? Try an immersion blender! Blending helps break up and evenly distribute the food coloring throughout the buttercream, which can help intensify the shade much faster. Just be careful not to overblend or warm it too much!
  • Try the microwave trick! If you have time to let your buttercream chill afterward, microwave a small portion for about 20 seconds, stir it back into the batch, and repeat as needed until you reach your desired color. Gently warming the buttercream can help the color develop faster. Just remember, it'll be too soft to decorate with immediately, so refrigerate it and remix once it's firm enough to work with.
  • Remember your butter! The natural yellow tint of buttercream can affect certain shades, especially lighter colors.

And here's one last reminder: don't rush to add more food coloring! Sometimes your buttercream just needs a little time or a little help developing that perfect shade.

Because let's be serious, good food coloring isn't cheap, and there's no reason to use half the bottle if you don't have to!

xo, Melinda
